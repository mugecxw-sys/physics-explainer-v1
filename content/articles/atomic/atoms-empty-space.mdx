<ShortAnswer>
Matter feels solid because the electron states in neighboring atoms strongly resist being compressed into the same arrangement, and electromagnetic interactions oppose further overlap. Solidity does not require atoms to be filled with tiny hard material.
</ShortAnswer>

## “Empty” does not mean inactive

The region outside a nucleus is not filled with miniature solid pieces, but it is where an atom’s electron states extend and where electromagnetic interactions occur. Calling that region empty can hide the physics that determines how atoms approach one another.

## What happens when you press on a surface

As atoms are pushed closer, their electron distributions interact. Electromagnetic repulsion and the quantum restrictions on identical electrons make further compression energetically costly. The surface pushes back.

<VisualExplanation title="Overlapping atomic regions resist compression">
  Solidity emerges from how neighboring quantum states and electric interactions respond as atomic regions are forced closer together.
</VisualExplanation>

## Why your hand does not pass through a table

The atoms in your hand and the table do not need to touch like rigid billiard balls. Their interactions create forces that redirect the motion of the atoms long before nuclei would meet.

<CommonMisconception>
“Atoms are mostly empty, so objects should pass through one another” treats empty space as the absence of forces and quantum rules. Those rules are exactly what determine the behavior of matter at ordinary distances.
</CommonMisconception>

## More precise version

The stability and rigidity of matter arise from electromagnetic interactions together with quantum mechanics, including the restrictions associated with the Pauli exclusion principle. Different materials respond differently because their structures and available electronic states differ.

## One thing to remember

Matter is solid because atoms resist being pushed into the same quantum arrangement, not because each atom is a completely filled hard sphere.
