<ShortAnswer>
Time dilation happens because space and time form a single geometric structure. Observers moving differently can take different paths through spacetime and therefore measure different amounts of elapsed time between events.
</ShortAnswer>

## Why your own clock feels normal

Time dilation never makes a nearby clock appear broken to the person traveling with it. Your heartbeat, thoughts, and watch all advance normally in your own frame. The difference appears when elapsed times from different paths or frames are compared.

## Light forces space and time to adjust together

All inertial observers measure the same speed of light. To preserve that shared measurement while observers disagree about distances and simultaneity, their measurements of space and time cannot remain universal and separate.

<VisualExplanation title="Different paths through spacetime">
  Two clocks can leave the same event, follow different paths through spacetime, and meet again having recorded different elapsed times.
</VisualExplanation>

## Why both observers can see the other clock as slow

During uniform relative motion, each observer can describe the other moving clock as running slowly. This symmetry is possible because the observers also disagree about which distant events happen at the same time. A reunion comparison requires considering the complete paths, including any change of motion.

<CommonMisconception>
Time dilation is not an optical delay or a clock malfunction. After signal-travel effects are accounted for, the difference in elapsed time remains a physical prediction that clocks can record.
</CommonMisconception>

## One thing to remember

Time dilation is a difference in elapsed time along different paths through spacetime, not a subjective feeling that time has slowed.
